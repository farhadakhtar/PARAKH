A bug that produced a working-looking result

Three of our five legal citations were wrong. The gate caught them.

Every compliance rule shipped locked — able to run its logic, unable to emit a finding — until someone checked its clause against the real document. We downloaded GFR 2017 from the Department of Expenditure and checked. Here is what came back:

What we had writtenWhat GFR 2017 actually says
Rule 155 = Advertised Tender Enquiry Rule 155 = purchase by committee, ₹25k–₹2.5L.
Advertised Tender is Rule 161.
Rule 154 = Limited Tender Enquiry Rule 154 = purchase without quotations, ≤ ₹25k.
Limited Tender is Rule 162.
Rule 230 = Utilisation Certificates Rule 230 = grants-in-aid principles.
UC timing is Rule 238 — and runs from financial-year close, not completion.
≥ 3 bidders for limited tender Rule 162 says “more than three” — so the minimum is four. Our check would have passed every 3-firm tender it exists to catch.

Had those rules been live, they would have flagged real public-works records against a legal basis that does not exist. Three are now verified against the source PDF and switched on; two stay locked.

What is blocked, and exactly why

Calibration cannot run — the years do not overlap

HARD BLOCK

Our only audit report and our only financial data describe different years. There is no join to make. This is not a modelling problem and no model fixes it.

audit evidence CAG SFAR, Government of Nagaland, FY 2020-21 financial data PMGSY state-year, FY 2023-24 · 24-25 · 25-26 · 26-27 year intersection EMPTY state intersection {NAGALAND} → one state work-level match impossible — no dataset carries a work ID

Bid data — SOLVED

RESOLVED

This was a hard block this morning. Two open datasets now supply the bid table PARAKH's schema never had, so all five typologies are reachable.

ProZorro Ukraine 240,000 bids, 2022-2025, CC-BY single-bidder share 50.8% 25% of losing bids within 0.04% of the winner EU cartel set 3.86M bids, 73 prosecuted cartels, CC-BY-NC 18,807 with a confirmed outcome still missing an INDIAN bid table. OECD guidance advises buyers not to publish losing bids, so an Indian public register is unlikely to ever carry one.

There are no negatives — and there cannot be without audit scope

HARD BLOCK

A calibration curve needs both outcomes. We can find what auditors flagged; we cannot find what they examined and cleared. Without that, “no finding” and “never audited” are the same row, and the honest label stays NULL.

confirmed issues findable from audit reports cleared records NOT findable — requires audit SCOPE, not findings result single-class data; no curve can be fitted

What we need — ranked by what it unlocks

HIGHEST VALUE

Tender-level bid data

From CPPP (eprocure.gov.in) or a state e-procurement portal. Unlocks the entire bid-rigging class — three typologies that are currently unreachable for want of data, not modelling.

CHEAPEST WIN

Two clause readings

Someone reads GFR Rule 238 and the CPWD Works Manual and confirms two clauses. That flips the last two compliance rules live. Roughly twenty minutes of a person who knows procurement.

FIXES COVERAGE

OMMAS district-level PMGSY

District or work level, FY 2020-21, covering Nagaland — matching the audit report we already hold. Closes the year gap that blocks every supervised experiment.

UNLOCKS CALIBRATION

Audit scope documents

Not findings — scope. Which units, districts and years an audit actually examined. This is the only thing that creates negatives, and therefore the only thing that makes calibration possible at all.

Read this before quoting any number

Nothing in PARAKH detects fraud today. The models being trained find records that contradict themselves and works that resemble each other. Both are useful — they tell an investigator where to look — and neither is an accusation.

The compliance rules find irregularity, which is also not fraud. An emergency repair done without tender breaks a rule and defrauds nobody. A flawless tender whose losing bidders are shells owned by the winner passes every check we have.

Every risk threshold in the system is still a stated judgement, labelled UNCALIBRATED, because calibrating one requires outcomes we do not have. That label stays until the blockers above are cleared.
