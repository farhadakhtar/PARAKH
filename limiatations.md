# Brutally Honest Systems Analysis: Fraud & Anomaly Detection for MPLADS and Comparable Public-Fund Schemes

## TL;DR
- **Data integrity — not analytical sophistication — is the binding constraint.** Every major CAG performance audit of MPLADS (1998, 2001, and Report No. 31 of 2010) documents that the underlying records are incomplete, unreconciled and gameable: in the 2010 audit, handing-over of assets was not on record for 14,828 of 15,049 sampled works (98.53%), dates were missing or invalid (some pre-dating the 1993 scheme), and ₹73.76 crore was spent on 2,340 prohibited works. An anomaly model layered on these records will learn reporting artifacts, not fraud. The highest-value AI intervention is therefore a **data-trust/verification layer**, not a fraud classifier.
- **The single largest genuine analytical gap is relationship blindness.** eSAKSHI, PFMS and every Indian scheme dashboard treat each work/payment as an independent record with no entity resolution and no graph/network analysis — so they structurally cannot see repeat-vendor concentration, shared bank accounts/addresses/directors, collusive clusters, or MP–agency–vendor conflicts of interest. The EU's ARACHNE tool and the US Recovery Operations Center prove this is tractable, but both depend on rich vendor/beneficial-ownership data that MPLADS public data does not expose.
- **A credible hackathon prototype can be built on genuinely open work-level data (Dataful/data.gov.in) — but only the 18th Lok Sabha (2024–) data carries vendor names, and no bank-account or payment-level records are public anywhere.** Teams should demonstrate entity resolution + graph analytics + unit-cost benchmarking + NLP work-deduplication on real open data, use unsupervised methods with audit-derived proxy labels (flagging the artifact-learning risk explicitly), and treat satellite verification as a narrow, honestly-caveated proof-of-concept given Sentinel-2's 10 m resolution versus culvert/borewell-scale assets.

## Key Findings

1. **The fund-flow chain is a chain of custody with weak links at every node.** MP recommendation (eSAKSHI) → District Authority sanction (45-day window) → Implementing Agency execution (12-month window) → vendor payment requests → "mark work complete." The District Authority (usually the District Collector/DM) holds the operative records; before 1 April 2023 these were physical bank accounts and paper registers maintained district-by-district, with no central work-level database.
2. **eSAKSHI (live 1 April 2023) digitises the workflow but not the verification.** It captures recommendations, sanctions, staged vendor-payment requests and stage-wise asset photographs, and shows real-time dashboards. But its public dashboard is aggregate-only, self-reported completion is the trigger for "completed" status, and the data is genuinely retrospective for everything before FY 2023-24 — the first four years of the 17th Lok Sabha (2019-20 to 2022-23) simply do not exist on the portal.
3. **PFMS tracks money, not merit.** It provides a transaction trail and unspent-balance visibility but does not validate whether an asset exists, whether a completion report is truthful, or whether two "independent" vendors are the same entity.
4. **The monitoring architecture is chronically under-resourced and self-policing.** The 2% administrative-expense cap funds monitoring; per MoSPI, "the District Authorities…shall inspect atleast 10% of the works under implementation every year…The State Nodal Department is also expected to inspect minimum 1% of MPLADS works in a District every year" — and audits repeatedly find even these minimal inspections are not documented.
5. **The category-(a)/(b) verdict is decisive and empirically settled: category (a) — data integrity — dominates.** This should reframe the SIH26102 solution from "fraud model" to "trust-and-verify layer with analytics on top."

## Details

### 1. Current System Architecture

**Fund-flow chain and data custody.** MPLADS (launched 23 December 1993; administered by MoSPI) gives each MP a ₹5 crore annual entitlement (raised from ₹2 crore to ₹5 crore in 2011-12), released in two ₹2.5 crore instalments. Lok Sabha MPs recommend works in their constituency; Rajya Sabha MPs in any district of their state; nominated members anywhere. The District Authority scrutinises, estimates cost, sanctions, selects the Implementing Agency (Panchayati Raj institutions are the preferred IA), and disburses. Data custody is fragmented: MoSPI holds release data; State nodal departments hold supervision records; District Authorities hold the operative work/sanction/expenditure records; Implementing Agencies hold execution and completion records. Before eSAKSHI this was overwhelmingly paper plus district bank accounts.

**eSAKSHI portal (live 1 April 2023, built with State Bank of India).** Per MoSPI/PIB and the portal's own public dashboard explainer:
- **Captures:** online MP recommendations and fund earmarking; District Authority sanctions; IA-raised staged vendor-payment requests; stage-wise asset photographs and sanction-order uploads; real-time role-based dashboards; a mobile app.
- **Timelines enforced:** 45 days for DA approval/decline; 12 months for IA completion; 18 months after an MP's tenure ends.
- **Does NOT capture / expose:** individual vendor identity or payment-level line items on the public dashboard (only summed "total vendor payments released" appear); any independent verification that an asset exists; anything before FY 2023-24. The portal confirms "the expenditure figures displayed on the dashboard represent the total vendor payments released against completed/ongoing works," and that pre-2023-24 recommendation/sanction data "is not available."
- **Completion is self-certified:** "Only those works that are marked as complete by the Implementing Agencies are reflected as completed works on the public dashboard." This is the central data-integrity weakness — the entity being monitored controls the completion signal.
- **Adoption:** all States/UTs onboarded; kiosks and a helpdesk operate during Parliament sessions. Reform reality: eSAKSHI closes the *timeliness/paper* gap and creates an audit trail, but it digitises the same self-reported-completion failure rather than closing it.

**PFMS (Public Financial Management System).** Originated as CPSMS (2009), renamed PFMS (2016), run by the Controller General of Accounts. It provides real-time fund-flow tracking to the last mile, unspent-balance MIS, and DBT rails integrated with 300+ banks' core banking. For MPLADS it can trace releases and balances, but it is a payments/accounting system: it does not validate asset existence, completion-report truthfulness, or vendor identity. Its own promoters concede it "cannot completely eliminate financial irregularities and corruption."

**Audit workflows and their cadence.** CAG performance audits of MPLADS are rare and lagged — 1998, 2001, and the major **Report No. 31 of 2010** (Performance Audit, covering 2004-09). The 2010 report noted the Ministry took until 2009 to file Action Taken Notes on the 2001 report — an eight-year lag. Statutory/district audit is delegated (State/UT engages auditors in consultation with the Accountant General). Physical verification is thinly mandated (DA ≥10%, State nodal ≥1%) and, per audits, poorly documented. The 2% administrative-expenses cap — split across nodal district, implementing districts and state nodal department — is the entire budget for monitoring staff, guaranteeing under-capacity at the district level where the real records live.

**Monitoring dashboards' analytical depth.** Descriptive only: funds released, funds available with DAs, works recommended/sanctioned/completed, expenditure, unspent/unsanctioned balances (see data.gov.in/Dataful field lists). There is no anomaly logic, no peer benchmarking, no network view — the dashboards report the very self-certified figures whose reliability the CAG questions.

**Comparable systems for benchmarking.**
- **NREGASoft / MGNREGS social audit.** The closest domestic analogue and a cautionary tale: a transaction-based real-time system (NREGASoft/NMMS) plus a statutory social-audit architecture (independent Social Audit Units, strongest in Andhra Pradesh and Meghalaya). Despite Aadhaar seeding (the Ministry of Rural Development reported 99.67% of active workers Aadhaar-seeded as of late 2025) and geo-tagged twice-daily photo attendance, fraud persists and migrates: Jharkhand's 2021-22 concurrent social audit found only 25.45% of muster-roll workers actually present at sites (40,629 of 1,59,608); Kerala's 2022 social audit flagged ~₹22 crore misappropriated in a partial sample. MoRD in July 2025 ordered *manual* re-verification of digital attendance after documented app manipulation — a direct lesson that digitisation of a gameable signal produces gameable digital records.
- **PFMS/DBT.** Strong on payment traceability and duplicate-beneficiary detection via Aadhaar (PM-KISAN below), weak on asset/works verification.
- **GeM.** Logs every procurement action with timestamps and runs some anomaly/analytics, but relies on seller self-declaration and OEM certification; documented residual risks are fake supplier registrations, sham firms, misuse of single-vendor/limited-tender exemptions, and off-portal evaluation. GeM's own roadmap lists AI fraud/anomaly detection as *planned*, not operational.
- **US Recovery Accountability and Transparency Board / Recovery Operations Center (2009-2015).** The pioneer. The ROC (led by IG Earl Devaney; ~35-80 staff; $84m board budget) maintained a database of Recovery Act contracts/grants and used graph-based link analysis (reportedly on Palantir) plus predictive risk indicators — prior criminal history, citizen tips, entity relationships under different names. Per GAO-15-814 (14 Sep 2015), "the center researched roughly 1.7 million entities associated with $36.4 billion in federal funds in fiscal years 2012 and 2013," identified organisations with prior fraud that nonetheless won Hurricane Sandy contracts, and GAO recommended preserving its capabilities. Critically, its data, resources and capabilities were **never transferred** to Treasury and the ROC shuttered in 2015 — a governance-failure lesson, not a technology one.
- **EU ARACHNE (the single most directly comparable operational system).** An EC data-mining/risk-scoring tool (developed since 2009) for ESF/ERDF/Cohesion (now CAP, RRF, etc.). It **ingests member-state operational data** (projects, payments, final recipients, beneficiaries, contracts, contractors, sub-contractors, beneficial owners, expenses) and **enriches** it with external commercial data (Orbis company data, WorldCompliance). Per the European Commission, "the Arachne Risk Scoring Tool identifies more than 100 risk indicators, which are grouped into 7 risk categories such as procurement, contract management, eligibility, performance, concentration, other and reputational and fraud alerts." Example indicators: number of companies registered at the same address; "high or deteriorating propensity to bankruptcy"; conflict-of-interest linkages; double-funding. By EC accounts the tool has been used by roughly 19 member states and has assessed over 1.2 million projects worth about €330 billion of EU funds — but **adoption has been uneven** because: it was voluntary for 2014-2020; accuracy depends on member states uploading complete data, which many do not; administrative burden and the prior existence of national tools reduced uptake (Poland built its own Cross-Check system; Germany a Competition Register); and data-confidentiality concerns were cited (e.g., Sweden, Finland). **The critique that matters most for SIH26102:** ARACHNE "does not supply any proof of error, irregularity or fraud" — it produces risk alerts requiring human verification. It is decision-support, not adjudication. It is being succeeded by ARACHNE+ (gradual replacement Apr–Jun 2026; full version end-2027), described as "AI-ready," with mandatory data provision only from the 2028-2034 financial framework.
- **Brazil CGU / Observatório da Despesa Pública (ODP).** A permanent strategic-information unit inside the CGU using IT-driven data mining to monitor public spending and generate fraud alerts, plus randomized lottery-based municipal audits with public photographic reports, and a transparency portal whose credit-card-data publication, per the GovLab/ODImpact case study, "led almost immediately to a 25 percent reduction in spending by officials on government cards." The World Bank's related **GRAS** (Governance Risk Assessment System, piloted in Brazil/Paraíba) uses ~60 red-flag indicators across four categories and during piloting flagged 850+ suppliers with collusion indications, ~450 likely strawmen, and ~500 cases of companies owned by public servants winning their own agency's contracts.
- **World Bank procurement red flags.** The INT "Fraud and Corruption Awareness Handbook" and related research catalogue red flags (same fax/phone/address across "competing" bidders, invoices exceeding contract value, unnecessary line items, single-source awards). A key empirical caveat from the literature ("Red Flags of Corruption in World Bank Projects"): many red flags are "too ubiquitous and randomly distributed to be useful," and only a subset (large tenders, lack of transparency, bidder collusion) reliably predict corruption — a direct warning against naive rule-based flagging.

### 2. Core Limitations

**A. Data limitations.** Fragmentation across DAs, IAs, PFMS, state treasuries and eSAKSHI; no real-time capture pre-2023 and retrospective entry even now; missing/unvalidated fields (the 2010 CAG found missing/invalid dates, some before 1993); no unified entity resolution across vendors/contractors/IAs/beneficiaries (same entity under multiple PANs/GSTINs/spellings/addresses); no persistent unique identifier for vendors (works now get a `unique_work_number` in the open data, but vendors do not); free-text `work_name` descriptions preventing reliable categorisation or duplicate detection.

**B. Analytical limitations.** Reliance on rule-based threshold checks and manual scrutiny; no statistical anomaly detection; no cross-transaction pattern recognition; purely reactive post-facto audit (years late) rather than predictive risk scoring; no peer benchmarking of unit costs across districts for comparable works.

**C. Relationship blindness (the most important technical gap).** Each work/payment is an independent record. No graph or network analysis exists anywhere in the MPLADS stack. Consequently the system cannot detect repeat-award concentration to the same vendor across MPs/districts; shared bank accounts, addresses, phone numbers or directors across nominally independent vendors; bid-rigging rings; circular fund flows; MP–IA–vendor conflicts of interest; or shell/dormant entities. ARACHNE, the ROC and GRAS all demonstrate this is exactly where operational systems add value.

**D. Temporal and behavioural limitations.** No detection of end-of-fiscal-year spending bursts (a well-documented Indian public-expenditure pattern), pre-election recommendation surges, abnormal sanction-to-completion intervals, threshold-evading split contracts, dormant-then-sudden vendor activity, or seasonal deviation from historical baselines.

**E. Investigation limitations.** No end-to-end case-management or investigation workflow; no fund-flow tracing from sanction to final payee (payment-level data isn't even public); no evidence-linked explanation of why something was flagged; no chain-of-custody for digital evidence; total dependence on manual auditors with no decision-support tooling; findings arriving years after expenditure. The ED/CBI cases below show investigations depend on seized phones and bank records obtained *after* the fact, not on any built-in tracing.

**F. Scalability issues.** ~543 Lok Sabha + ~245 Rajya Sabha members at ₹5 crore/year each (~₹3,900+ crore annual entitlement) across ~19.86 lakh cumulative completed works (₹54,171.09 crore since inception, per MoSPI). Audits sample a tiny fraction; reporting cycles are slow; detection latency is measured in years.

**G. Human-factor problems.** Cognitive overload; the 2% administrative cap starving district monitoring; alert fatigue/false positives (the red-flag ubiquity problem); acute political sensitivity of flagging works recommended by sitting legislators; institutional incentives against reporting; and auditor skill gaps in data analytics.

### 3. Real-World Failure Scenarios (documented vs alleged)

- **CAG Report No. 31 of 2010 (Performance Audit, 2004-09) — documented audit findings:** ₹73.76 crore on 2,340 prohibited works across 100 sampled districts; handing-over of assets not on record for 14,828 of 15,049 sampled works (98.53%; in six of seven states studied, *none* had handing-over records); missing/invalid dates including pre-1993 entries; ₹53.74 crore spent on works inadmissible under the scheme; 775 sanctioned works (₹10.18 crore) never taken up; expenditure persistently below available funds. These are category-(a) data-integrity failures.
- **CAG Report No. 4 of 2018 — documented audit finding, politically sensitive:** the CAG reported payment of ₹5.93 crore without inviting tenders and ₹84.53 lakh to a cooperative society under MPLAD funds associated with a sitting minister; opposition parties demanded prosecution. Report this strictly as a documented CAG audit finding that became politically contested; it is not an adjudicated conviction, and it should not be asserted as proven wrongdoing against the named individual.
- **COVID-19 suspension (2020-2022):** MPLADS was suspended from 6 April 2020. Per Union I&B Minister Anurag Thakur (Nov 2021), "In April 2020, the government had decided to divert MPLADS funds for two years – 2020-21 and 2021-22 – totalling about Rs 7,900 crore to improve health infrastructure and manage health services in the wake of Covid-19 pandemic." It was restored for the remainder of 2021-22 (₹2 crore/MP) and continued at ₹5 crore/MP through FY 2025-26. Effect: a multi-year data discontinuity — the eSAKSHI record begins mid-scheme, with no work-level history for the first four years of the 17th Lok Sabha.
- **Unspent-balance/UC failures (recent):** Assam's unspent MPLAD balance was ₹41 crore as of March 2023, plus ₹42 crore unreleased due to district authorities' failure to submit progress reports and utilisation certificates. Standing/Estimates Committee reports document routine UC/audit-certificate relaxations and the Ministry's own admission it does not reliably maintain records — a direct admission of category-(a) failure.
- **MGNREGS analogue (closest well-documented fraud pattern):** ghost workers, fake muster rolls, contractor infiltration; Jharkhand 2021-22 (25.45% attendance), Kerala 2022 (~₹22 crore), and a Bihar ICT-sabotage episode.
- **PM-KISAN ineligible-beneficiary recoveries:** ₹416.75 crore recovered nationally (Minister's Lok Sabha reply, Dec 2025); an earlier parliamentary answer put ineligible transfers at ₹4,352 crore by March 2022 with only ₹297 crore recovered; Kangra district alone issued recovery notices to 4,189 ineligible recipients for ₹5.72 crore. Demonstrates that even Aadhaar/PFMS-integrated schemes leak, and that cross-database matching (Income Tax, PDS, UIDAI) is what catches duplicates/ineligibles.
- **MLA-LAD / corporation fund diversion (adjudication ongoing):** the Karnataka Maharshi Valmiki ST Development Corporation case (ED/CBI/SIT; alleged routing of department funds through intermediary firms) illustrates the circular-fund-flow and strawman-entity patterns MPLADS analytics would target — but these remain under investigation, not adjudicated, and named individuals should not be described as guilty.

### 4. Gap Summary (ranked by severity × AI-tractability)

1. **Current systems cannot verify that a claimed completed asset physically exists** — completion is self-certified by the paying entity. (Highest severity; partially AI-tractable via geospatial + photo forensics, but hard.)
2. **There is no entity resolution and no graph analysis anywhere in the stack** — so no system can link vendors sharing accounts/addresses/directors or detect award concentration and collusion. (High severity; highly AI-tractable *if* vendor data exists — it only does for 18th LS.)
3. **Investigators lack any fund-flow tracing to the final payee and any case-management/evidence-linking tooling.** (High severity; tractable as tooling, blocked by data access.)
4. **Current systems cannot benchmark unit costs for comparable works across districts.** (Medium-high severity; highly AI-tractable on existing open work-level data.)
5. **There is no temporal/behavioural anomaly detection** (year-end bursts, split contracts, dormant-then-active vendors). (Medium severity; tractable on existing data.)
6. **Current systems cannot categorise or de-duplicate free-text work descriptions.** (Medium severity; highly AI-tractable via NLP on open data.)
7. **Investigators lack labelled fraud data**, forcing unsupervised methods and audit-derived proxy labels — with the inherent risk of learning reporting artifacts. (Fundamental constraint, not a gap AI can "close.")

### 5. Opportunity for AI (where it helps — and where it does NOT)

- **Graph analytics + entity resolution (highest analytical value).** Build a vendor/IA/beneficiary graph; resolve entities across name-spelling/address/phone/account variants; surface concentration, shared-attribute clusters, and MP–IA–vendor conflict triangles. *Data needed:* vendor identifiers, addresses, bank/PAN/GSTIN. *Reality:* vendor names are public only for the 18th LS (Dataful 22565, work↔vendor↔amount); bank/PAN/GSTIN are **not public**. So a 36-hour demo can do entity resolution and graph clustering on 18th-LS vendor-name data (plus synthetic attribute augmentation), but true beneficial-ownership linkage needs MCA/GSTN access that only the Ministry can grant. *This is where ARACHNE/ROC/GRAS get their power — and precisely where MPLADS open data is thinnest.*
- **Unsupervised anomaly detection under near-total label scarcity (fundamental issue — address head-on).** With essentially no labelled fraud, supervised learning is infeasible; the literature (Isolation Forest, autoencoders, pseudo-labeling) shows unsupervised structural-deviation detection plus **proxy labels derived from CAG/social-audit findings** is the defensible path. **But the decisive caveat:** because the source records are themselves unreliable (self-certified completions, missing dates, retrospective entry), an anomaly detector will disproportionately learn *reporting artifacts* (e.g., which districts enter data late) rather than fraud. Proxy labels from audit findings are approximate and must be validated against ground truth. This is why the honest recommendation is a trust-layer first.
- **Unit-cost peer benchmarking (best hackathon-to-production ratio).** Cluster comparable works (via NLP on `work_name`) and flag unit-cost outliers across districts. *Data:* fully available in open work-level datasets (sanction_amount per unique_work_number). Demonstrable in 36 hours; low data risk.
- **Temporal/change-point/burst detection.** Detect year-end sanction bursts, pre-election surges, abnormal sanction-to-completion intervals. *Data:* dates and amounts are in the open datasets. Demonstrable, moderate false-positive burden.
- **NLP on free-text work descriptions.** Categorise works into a controlled taxonomy and detect duplicate/near-duplicate works (same work claimed twice, as the 2018 CAG finding alleged). *Data:* available. High value, demonstrable.
- **Satellite/geospatial verification (highest-value data-integrity intervention — but assess honestly).** This directly attacks the self-certification weakness by independently checking asset existence. **Feasibility is genuinely limited:** free Sentinel-2 is 10 m resolution (2-5 day revisit), adequate for large land-cover change but *unable to resolve* culverts, borewells, single community halls, or small road segments — the typical MPLADS work. Verification is realistic only for larger footprints (buildings, ponds, road stretches) or via change-detection (before/after new construction), and reliably only with commercial sub-metre imagery (0.3-0.5 m) or super-resolution methods that add cost and error. A 36-hour demo can show change-detection on a handful of larger assets with public imagery; genuine operational verification needs commercial imagery + drone/geo-tagged-photo EXIF cross-checks and is a longer program. Do **not** overclaim satellite verification of small assets.
- **Explainable risk scoring (audit-defensibility requirement).** Any score used against a sitting legislator's works must be explainable and evidence-linked (like ARACHNE's per-indicator pop-ups), producing "why flagged" narratives — never an automated verdict. ARACHNE's own charter ("does not supply any proof of error") is the model.
- **Case-management/investigation tooling.** Where AI adds least novelty but most operational value: linking flags to evidence, chain-of-custody, workflow. Buildable, blocked mainly by data access.

**Where AI does NOT help:** it cannot manufacture missing utilisation certificates, cannot make a self-certified completion true, cannot resolve entities without identifiers, and cannot substitute for the physical inspection the guidelines already mandate but that rarely happens. On unreliable source data, more sophisticated models mostly produce more confident artifacts.

### 6. Data Availability Assessment (for system builders)

**Genuinely available and downloadable now:**
- **Work-level records (real, free-format CSV/XLSX/Parquet) via Dataful (data sourced from mplads.gov.in/MoSPI):** 17th LS list of works (dataset 18533, ~73,305 rows, 17 columns), 16th LS (18534), 15th LS (18535), sitting Rajya Sabha (18539, ~13,638 rows), former RS (18531). **Fields include:** state, nodal_district, implementing_district, constituency, house_name, member_type, mp_name, sanction_amount, date_of_administrative_approval, work_name (free text), unique_work_number, implementing_agency_name, work_status, date_of_receipt_of_work_proposal_from_mp, unit. **This supports unit-cost benchmarking, NLP categorisation/dedup, temporal analysis, and IA-level concentration — immediately.**
- **Fund-summary datasets (aggregate, not work-level):** 18540, 18541 (state-wise), 18542 (per-MP entitled/released/sanctioned/unspent).
- **Vendor-level data — ONLY for the 18th Lok Sabha (eSAKSHI era):** Dataful dataset 22565 ("State, Lok Sabha Constituency, District, Implementing Agency Name, MP Name, Work Name, Vendor Name" + amount spent), with companions 22566/22567 (work-name-level totals, no vendor). Caveat: some 18th-LS datasets are premium-tier ("HFI"-tagged); the exact 22565 column headers could not be verbatim-confirmed.
- **eSAKSHI public dashboard (mplads.mospi.gov.in):** aggregate counts and expenditure totals + stage-wise asset photos behind stakeholder logins; **not** a downloadable granular table.
- **data.gov.in** keyword pages for MPLADS exist (publisher MoSPI) but resource-level fields could not be verified due to access restrictions.
- **CAG report annexures** (cag.gov.in) and MoSPI annual reports / MPLADS guidelines (2016 and April 2023 editions on mplads.gov.in) for context and proxy-label mining.

**NOT available publicly (anywhere verifiable):**
- Vendor **bank account details** — none, in any dataset.
- **Payment-level / installment-level transaction records** — none; only "amount spent" totals per work/vendor.
- Vendor **PAN/GSTIN/directors/beneficial ownership** — none in MPLADS data (would require MCA21/GSTN linkage).
- Any **work-level or vendor data before FY 2023-24 for the eSAKSHI era**, and no vendor names for 17th LS and earlier.

**Verdict on prototype feasibility:** A credible prototype **can** be built on genuinely open data for four of the highest-value capabilities — unit-cost benchmarking, NLP work-categorisation/deduplication, temporal-burst detection, and IA/vendor concentration graphs (vendor names for 18th LS; IA names throughout). For beneficial-ownership-grade collusion detection (the ARACHNE/GRAS class), the required vendor-attribute data does not exist publicly, so teams should (a) demonstrate the graph/entity-resolution engine on 18th-LS vendor-name data, and (b) use a **defensible synthetic-data strategy** — synthesise vendor bank/PAN/address/director attributes with realistic collusion patterns (shared accounts, circular flows) *calibrated to the World Bank/ARACHNE red-flag typologies and Brazilian GRAS findings*, clearly labelled as synthetic, to prove the detection logic while being explicit that production requires MCA/GSTN/PFMS data access from the Ministry.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Reframe the problem (do this first).** Build the solution as a **data-trust and verification layer with analytics on top**, not a standalone fraud classifier. The evidence (CAG 2010, Estimates Committee, Assam UC failures, MGNREGS digitisation experience) is unambiguous that data integrity is the binding constraint. *Benchmark that would change this:* if the Ministry grants access to reconciled, validated eSAKSHI work+vendor+payment records with unique vendor IDs, shift weight toward the analytical/graph layer.

**Stage 1 — Hackathon-demonstrable (36 hours), on real open data.** (1) Unit-cost outlier benchmarking per work-type cluster; (2) NLP categorisation of free-text `work_name` + near-duplicate detection; (3) temporal burst/change-point detection on sanction dates; (4) IA/vendor concentration graph on 18th-LS vendor-name data. Ship with an **explainable "why flagged"** panel per item (ARACHNE-style) and an honest false-positive dashboard.

**Stage 2 — Graph + entity resolution demo with synthetic augmentation.** Entity-resolution engine on 18th-LS vendors; graph detection of shared-attribute clusters, circular flows and conflict triangles on synthetic-but-typology-calibrated attribute data, explicitly labelled synthetic.

**Stage 3 — Narrow geospatial proof-of-concept.** Change-detection on a handful of *larger* assets (buildings/ponds/road stretches) using public imagery + geo-tagged-photo EXIF cross-check; state plainly that culvert/borewell-scale verification needs commercial sub-metre imagery or drones.

**Stage 4 — Production preconditions (name them to the sponsor).** Unique persistent vendor IDs in eSAKSHI; PFMS payment-level integration; MCA21/GSTN/Income-Tax linkage for beneficial ownership; mandatory geo-tagged, EXIF-preserved completion photos; and a proxy-label pipeline mining CAG/social-audit findings — validated against a small human-audited ground-truth set. *Benchmark:* target precision on audit-confirmed cases, not raw anomaly counts.

**Governance guardrail.** Given named sitting legislators, mandate human-in-the-loop adjudication, explainability, and an appeals/challenge window (GeM-style). The system flags risk for verification; it never adjudicates fraud.

## Caveats
- **Data-integrity dominance is an evidence-based judgment**, drawn primarily from CAG performance audits (notably Report No. 31 of 2010) and parliamentary committee findings; the 2010 audit predates eSAKSHI, and no post-eSAKSHI CAG performance audit of comparable depth was located, so eSAKSHI-era integrity is partly inferred from portal design (self-certified completion) rather than a fresh audit.
- **Politically sensitive cases** (CAG Report No. 4 of 2018; Karnataka Valmiki corporation) are reported strictly as documented audit findings or ongoing investigations, not proven wrongdoing against named individuals.
- **Member counts** (~543 Lok Sabha, ~245 Rajya Sabha) and the ₹5 crore entitlement are current per MoSPI/PIB; the entitlement runs through the 15th Finance Commission period and could be revised.
- **Dataful dataset 22565's exact columns** could not be verbatim-confirmed (dynamic-page access blocked); its vendor-name granularity is inferred from title + collection metadata, and some 18th-LS datasets may be paywalled.
- **Sentinel-2 feasibility** figures are from ESA/remote-sensing literature; the 10 m limit versus small MPLADS assets is a hard physical constraint, not a modelling gap.
- **ARACHNE usage figures** (~19 member states, ~1.2 million projects, ~€330 billion) are from a European Commission presentation and should be read as EC-reported, not independently audited.
- Several secondary facts rely on reputable Indian journalism (Deccan Herald, Business Standard, The Tribune, Sentinel Assam) and explainer sites; primary CAG/PIB/MoSPI sources were preferred wherever available.
